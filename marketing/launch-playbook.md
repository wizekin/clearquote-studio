## 1. Customer acquisition: start with useful content

Share useful, standalone information instead of posting repeated advertisements. Free resources from the website include:
- A **free project pricing calculator** (hours, rate, costs, contingency, discount, tax and deposit).
- A **free deposit calculator** (upfront amount, remaining balance).
- **How to price a freelance project without guessing**.
- **Quote vs. invoice: what to send and when**.
- **How to calculate a freelance deposit**.

Publish only where the community permits links and self-promotion. Avoid mass DMs, automated unsolicited comments and claims about guaranteed sales. Do not fabricate testimonials or customers.

## 3. Practical places to look for feedback

- Relevant freelance and small-business communities that explicitly allow constructive self-promotion.
- Direct feedback from a handful of willing freelancers you already know (with no pressure to buy).
- Useful answers on public Q&A forums, with links only when they genuinely solve the question and comply with site rules.
- Search-engine-indexable guides on the website (long-term, uncertain results; indexing and traffic are not guaranteed).

## 4. Measurement without fabricated numbers

Record per week: website visits (from an actual hosting analytics provider), clicks to Gumroad if your platform measures them, real successful sales, gross revenue, refunds, fee deductions, and net earnings.

- **Conversion rate** = real completed orders ÷ real website sessions or attributed checkout visits. State which denominator you use; **do not mix unmatched data sources**.
- **Gross revenue** = sum of actual transaction prices; zero sales means $0 in observed revenue.
- **Revenue per visitor** = attributable revenue ÷ measured visits, if attribution is available.

Track where visitors come from by source tags on the buy links (`utm_source=clearquote_site`, `utm_medium=website`, `utm_campaign=launch`). UTM tags are not themselves a visitor analytics system, and whether a purchase is attributed depends on reporting capabilities and privacy settings.

## 5. Decision checkpoints

- **After 1–2 weeks:** confirm the site is reachable, guides render, the free calculator works and Gumroad's test checkout remains correct.
- **After the first meaningful traffic:** ask visitors what they expected from the product, whether the $3 price makes sense, and which functionality would make them use it regularly.
- **After the first sales:** compare real revenue, fees and refunds; identify the best-performing referral channels.
- **Before raising prices:** improve differentiation—project risk, scope revisions, profitability and client-ready document templates—then re-evaluate rather than assuming a higher price is justified.

**Do not store actual purchaser emails, financial credentials, or private support correspondence in the public GitHub repository.**
