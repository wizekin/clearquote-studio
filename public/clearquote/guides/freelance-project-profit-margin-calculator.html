How to calculate freelance project profit margin

Subtract your work-time cost, direct expenses, allocated overhead and transaction fees from the agreed project price. Then divide the remainder by the project price to estimate profit margin. Your real accounting profit and tax liability depend on additional circumstances.

Formula: Estimated project profit = price − (hours × value of your time) − direct costs − allocated overhead − payment fees. Margin: estimated project profit ÷ price × 100, when price is greater than zero.

For example, on a $1,500 project requiring 20 hours of your time valued at $35/hour, plus $250 expenses, $75 allocated overhead and a 3% transaction fee, your time cost is $700, fees are $45 and total estimated costs are $1,070. That leaves $430 in estimated profit, or a 28.7% margin, before income taxes and other unmodeled costs.

Why margin and markup are different

Margin compares profit to what the client pays. Markup compares profit to your cost. If a project costs $800 and you quote $1,000, the profit is $200: a 20% margin, but a 25% markup on cost. Confusing these two measures can lead to underpricing.

How to choose a target quote

To estimate a price that supports your chosen margin after percentage-based payment fees, this tool divides your base costs plus fixed fees by 1 − fee percentage − target margin percentage. If the fee percentage and target margin add to 100% or more, the selected target cannot be achieved with this model.

Things a project margin calculator cannot predict

  • Unexpected revisions: extra hours lower the profit even if the client price remains fixed.
  • Tax, bad debt and currency changes: these require separate planning and may differ by country.
  • Unpaid sales time: proposals and administrative hours also have a cost; include them in your estimated work time or overhead.
  • Overhead allocation: your monthly software, equipment and business costs should not be ignored.

Frequently asked questions

Can my estimated profit be negative?

Yes. If labor, expenses and fees exceed the quoted price, the calculator displays a negative amount and negative margin. That signals the assumptions don't support the quote.

Is the hourly earnings number the same as profit per hour?

No. It is the amount left per work hour after non-time expenses and fees but before subtracting your assigned labor cost. The headline profit estimate then subtracts the value you assigned to your time.

Should I include my own work as a project cost?

Yes, if you want to see whether a project generates an additional margin after compensating yourself for your time. You can also use the free hourly-rate calculator to understand your baseline earning requirements.

How should I handle deposits?

Deposits normally change cash-flow timing, not the agreed project price or total profit. Use the deposit calculator to split payments.
