Quote vs. Invoice: What Freelancers Should Send and When

Learn the practical difference between a client quote, an accepted estimate, and an invoice.

A quote and an invoice are different documents. Both can contain client names, itemized work, prices and payment terms, but they appear at different stages of a project.

What is a quote?

A quote (or estimate, depending on your business practices and local rules) sets out proposed work and its expected price before the client agrees to proceed. A useful quote identifies the deliverables, quantities or scope, prices, taxes where applicable, project assumptions, validity period, and acceptance steps.

What is an invoice?

An invoice requests payment for work or a contractually agreed milestone. It commonly includes the parties' details, an invoice number, issue date, description of work, amount due, due date and payment instructions. Legal requirements differ by jurisdiction; a general tool cannot verify compliance for you.

Simple example

A client asks for a logo package. You first send a quote offering three logo concepts and two revisions for $450. Once you both agree, you begin work under those terms. When the first milestone or delivery occurs, you send an invoice requesting the payment due under the agreement.

What about deposits?

Some freelancers request an upfront deposit or milestone payment before starting. Document the amount, when it is due, what it is applied toward and what happens if the project is cancelled. A deposit is part of the total project payment, not an extra charge unless clearly agreed.

Practical checklist

  • Use consistent client and business information.
  • Describe the deliverables in plain language.
  • Clearly separate estimated price from amount currently due.
  • State currencies, taxes and deadlines explicitly.
  • Keep a copy of acceptance and payment records.
